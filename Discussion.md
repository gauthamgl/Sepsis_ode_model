# 4 Discussion

## 4.1 Genetic Associations with Sepsis

### 4.1.1 Genomic Context and Lineage Characteristics
Before exploring specific genetic associations, it is critical to contextualise these findings within the broader population structure of the isolates. Our genomic profiling confirmed a high degree of genetic diversity across the 200 *Klebsiella pneumoniae* genomes (72 sequence types), yet revealed a distinct lineage segregation between the clinical phenotypes. The sepsis cohort was dominated by high-risk international clones—such as ST512, ST101, and ST147—whereas the non-sepsis group was predominantly composed of ST15 isolates [57].

This lineage segregation was mirrored by a profound and widespread antimicrobial resistance burden. While baseline resistance to β-lactams, fosfomycin, and fluoroquinolones, along with the prevalence of the CTX-M-15 extended-spectrum β-lactamase, was pervasive across the entire dataset, the sepsis cohort was distinguished by an even higher density of acquired resistance genes. Most notably, the clear enrichment of carbapenemases (*bla*_KPC and *bla*_NDM) among the sepsis isolates highlights their pronounced adaptation to the therapeutic pressures of the hospital environment [58].

This heavy multidrug-resistant profile provides a crucial interpretive framework for the subsequent GWAS results. In highly clonal bacterial populations, these prevalent resistance elements serve as powerful biomarkers for healthcare-associated lineages [55]. Their prominence in the sepsis cohort suggests that their invasive success in hospital settings is inextricably linked to their ability to survive clinical interventions. Consequently, the resistance-linked signals identified in the association analysis—rather than acting as direct causative drivers of physical invasiveness—reflect the intense environmental adaptation of these classical, hospital-endemic *K. pneumoniae* strains.

### 4.1.2 Primary and Secondary GWAS Signals
The study has reported the development of a mathematical genotype model for sepsis caused by gram negative bacteria, providing a means to link the findings of the pangenome wide association study with the immune dynamics of sepsis by mapping PySEER mGWAS effect sizes into ODE rate constants. In this two stage association analysis the dominant primary signals (baseline) were separated from the independent secondary signals (conditional), and the principle biological theme exhibited by the sepsis enriched isolates from this study was the tolerance to host induced stress. The strongest signals identified from the baseline analysis was the clpK/clpB chaperone operon and the ergothioneine biosynthesis (egtB) clusters from the conditional analysis, both of which help the bacteria survive the oxidative and proteotoxic pressure from host phagocytes even though they work through different mechanisms. The convergence between these two independent genes makes the finding more reliable, as a single gene would produce a false association to sepsis due to shared ancestry. However, it is very unlikely for two independent genes to converge at the same survival mechanism by chance. Similarly, the identification of the two clpK genes through baseline analysis strengthens the argument as the isolated clpK gene was non sepsis enriched whereas the clpK_1/clpB_1/clpK chaperone operon was sepsis enriched. This indicates that the clpB along the DnaK (Hsp70) system, reactivates aggregated proteins and thereby supports the survival of bacteria under host induced oxidative stress and heat [28,30]. Hence its co-occurrence with clpB gene appears to be the association with sepsis infection [25]. The conditional analysis itself was a key finding as it produced eight new secondary signals. One of these was the antioxidant ergothioneine signal which was identified only after conditioning the baseline hits, emphasising the importance of conditional genome wide association study for revealing the secondary associations which were masked by the primary baseline signals [13,14]. Together these clusters are responsible for the survival of extreme conditions such as proteotoxic and oxidative pressures encountered within the host bloodstream and within phagocytes. However, these signals must be interpreted against the population structure of the species. The genomic inflation remained elevated even after the kinship correction by MASH based matrix (λ = 1.531 baseline; λ = 1.460 conditional) which is a recognised and expected feature in bacterial GWAS. This is because Klebsiella pneumoniae reproduces clonally, where causal and non-causal variants are linked on a shared clonal frame, so the lineage effects can increase the test statistics and provide associations which reflect the shared ancestry rather than gene specific effects [10,13]. Hence, the two stage analysis was performed to address this reason and along with the biological convergence of the signals it is unlikely that these signals arise from ancestry alone although residual confounding cannot be entirely eliminated in mGWAS studies[9]. 

### 4.1.3 Stress Tolerance vs. Classical Virulence
The sepsis associated genes found in this study relate to stress tolerance rather than to the classical virulence factors of Klebsiella pneumoniae. In hypervirulent strains, invasiveness is largely driven by capsule overproduction and by siderophores such as aerobactin, which are usually carried on a large virulence plasmid and are associated with community acquired infections in healthy individuals outside hospitals [47]. In this study, the sepsis isolates reflect the classical, hospital associated strains, in which the outcome of the infection is shaped by the interplay of the resistance and fitness genes distributed across genetically distinct lineages [2]. This interpretation is supported by the genomic characterisation of the isolates, in which classical virulence determinants were uncommon and were not frequent in sepsis isolates when compared to non sepsis controls, confirming that the invasive phenotype was not driven by the hypervirulence determinants of Klebsiella pneumoniae. The study results producing ergothioneine biosynthesis and chaperone mediated proteostasis, rather than capsule or siderophore determinants, as the leading sepsis associated signals suggests that the ability to survive the oxidative and proteotoxic stress of the phagocytes distinguishes the invasive isolates more clearly than the usual hypervirulence factors do.

### 4.1.4 Biomarkers of Invasive Lineages
A second group of baseline hits were identified from the analysis such as beta lactamase genes and IS6 family transposase, which are represented as biomarkers that reflect the genomic characteristics of the invasive lineages rather than the main driver of sepsis. For example, in clonal bacterial species such as Klebsiella pneumoniae, genes which are linked to invasive lineages show strong association with invasion even though they are not responsible for it[2]. Resistance genes are a prominent example as they increase the patient mortality by reducing the treatment options due to the presence of antimicrobial resistance genes but not by making the bacteria more invasive [31]. Supporting these findings, the AMRfinderplus profiling showed that multidrug resistant, carbapenemase enriched characteristics was common among the sepsis isolates, supporting the idea that the resistance associated signals obtained here (bla_1, IS6) are the biomarkers of the healthcare associated invasive lineages rather than primary drivers of invasion. This interpretation is strengthened by the study design where the sepsis and non-sepsis isolates were obtained from different public repositories and varied in lineage composition, so the virulence and resistance differences mentioned are interpreted as the features of genetic characteristics rather than the primary drivers of invasion. 

### 4.1.5 Non-sepsis Enriched Signals and Future Directions
The non-sepsis enriched signals from conditional analysis were dominated by conjugative plasmid backbone genes. Group_4547 encodes a FinO family fertility inhibition protein which is involved in the regulation of conjugative transfer of F like plasmids [38] and the klcA cluster encodes an antirestriction protein enabling the plasmids to evade the type I restriction modification systems of the host and forms a part of the bla_KPC plasmid backbone in Klebsiella pneumoniae [39]. These genes play an important role in maintaining the plasmid structure but do not contribute to sepsis infection. Additionally, it was found that group_4215 and group_5443 were shown to share identical allele frequencies, p values and effect sizes with the group_4547 (FinO family) and klcA cluster respectively. This suggests that they are co-located on the same mobile elements and represent a single signal rather than independent association. The remaining signals produced were largely annotated as hypothetical proteins and cannot be interpreted from sequence alone. From the baseline hits, group_5402 is another hypothetical protein with a weak sepsis association (β = +0.328) but carries no functional annotation. Similarly, group_3230 and group_786 were also non sepsis enriched hypothetical proteins but due to the lack of functional annotation they remain as candidates for future studies. The interpretation of these signals is limited by the cohort size (100 sepsis, 100 non-sepsis) as the statistical power to detect small effect associations is reduced. However, this analysis is performed to detect only the strong effect associations [48]. Therefore, the interpretations mentioned above are the hypothesis for future studies and functional validation such as targeted gene knockout, survival assays under oxidative, thermal stress and in macrophage killing would be required to confirm any association to the sepsis pathogenesis. However the bacterial specific nature of clpB, ergothioneine biosynthesis (egtB) and their absence from the human cells represents these bacterial stress tolerance systems as strong promising targets for the selective elimination of invasive Klebsiella pneumoniae with minimal host toxicity [28,34].

## 4.2 Mathematical Modeling of Genetic Effects

### 4.2.1 Global Sensitivity Analysis
The global sensitivity analysis underscores the critical dependence of sepsis progression on unrestricted bacterial replication and immune evasion. The strong influence of the PySEER derived multipliers stress and ROS highlights the mechanistic importance of the bacterial genetic background in determining the severity of the host response. 

A key concern addressed in this study was whether the qualitative model predictions were overly sensitive to the mathematical translation of statistical GWAS effect sizes into mechanistic ODE multipliers. While the 10 mapping produced more extreme values that clustered at the upper simulation bound Dmax=1.50, all mappings consistently predicted that the invasive genotype combinations possess a strong baseline propensity for causing sepsis. This consistency demonstrates that the core conclusions drawn from the integrated PySEER ODE framework are highly reliable and do not depend on the default exponential mapping choice.

### 4.2.2 Translating Genetic Associations into the Model
Translating these associations into the model, egtB acts through a stable thiol with excellent superoxide- and hydrogen-peroxide-scavenging ability that diverse gram-negative pathogens synthesise in response to neutrophil oxidative bursts; in the model it reduces effective neutrophil ROS-mediated killing by a factor of 1.48. In a macrophage co-culture assay with NADPH oxidase activity, the egtB mutant exhibited significantly higher fitness than wild-type M. tuberculosis and P. aeruginosa [40,42].

The clpK/clpB operon, the highest single-locus β-contributor on our array, encodes AAA+ ATPase disaggregases (Hsp104/ClpB) that solubilise and renature protein aggregates formed under heat-shock, oxidative, and antimicrobial stress. Although ClpB and ClpK are distinct, both disaggregases use a common disaggregase threading-channel mechanism shown to facilitate intracellular survival and proliferation within macrophages by Legionella pneumophila, Brucella abortus, and enterohaemorrhagic Escherichia coli [1]. In the proposed model, the multiplier φ_stress = 1.54 reduces the macrophage killing effect, reflecting the capacity of clpK/clpB proteins to defend against macrophage induced protein misfolding and degradation stress. As observed previously, clpK/clpB proteins show the highest contribution in tissue damage (Dmax = 0.313 for clpK/clpB versus Dmax = 0.283 for egtB), which indicates delayed phase immunopathology caused by impaired macrophage killing, where surviving bacterial cells recruit inflammatory cells, resulting in more DAMP production and tissue injury [1].

As reported above, neither the development of AMR nor transposable genes are related to tissue damage or cytokine storm in the absence of antibiotic simulation; instead, their virulence is wholly dependent on the therapeutic pressure applied against bacteria. The phiabx = 2.09 multiplier, obtained based on the sum of the updated mGWAS =+0.738, results in shifting the bactericidal EC50 to the right, thereby significantly raising the effective MIC. Co-expression of IS6100 and bla_1 is expected due to the common genetic architecture characteristic of ESBL-producing gram-negative bacteria.
 
## 4.3 Immune Dynamics, Tissue Damage, and Antibiotic Therapy

### 4.3.1 The Role of IL-6 and TNF-α in Cytokine Storms
The amplificatory node that IL-6 plays, together with TNF-α, in the cytokine cascade is a fundamental point for the understanding of CARS. In particular, the fast rise (normalised IL-6 + TNF-α ≈ 0.35–0.42 at ~6 h) although representing an excellent match with the experimental endotoxaemic model conducted in humans, in which IL-6 shows a peak after two to four hours post LPS recognition and then decreases in 24 hours due to receptor mediated uptake and negative regulation of the cascade through glucocorticoids and IL-10 [42], reflects only partially the role played by the secondary IL-10 induction term (kIL10,cyt = 0.80) in producing the anti inflammatory effect typical of the CARS state. The failure of the fully invasive untreated scenario to achieve cytokine resolution (persistent secondary IL-6 waves from day 4) is consistent with the clinical entity of cytokine storm in non resolving sepsis. The non resolving nature of the fully invasive model in the absence of therapy can be considered an analogous case to cytokine storm in sepsis, in which, due to the continuous antigenic stimulation provided by the live bacteria, the negative regulation mechanism fails.

### 4.3.2 IL-10 Kinetics and Immunosuppression
The biphasic IL-10 kinetics is simulated in this model. The first phase, rapid induction during the peak of pro inflammatory cytokines followed by second phase increase in case of persistent infections resembles the CARS pattern of human sepsis [43] whereby the initial SIRS reaction followed by prolonged immunosuppression characterised by decreased HLA-DR expression, impaired antigen presenting capacity, and T cell exhaustion [43,44]. The mechanism of IL-10 induced immunosuppression involves application of a 0.55 suppression factor to all bactericidal functions and cytokine synthesis reflecting pleiotropic effect of IL-10 in down regulating neutrophil production of ROS, macrophage phagocytic activity, and cytokine gene expression. It should be noted that the model structure implies that IL-10 induced immunosuppression leaves open the chance for bacteria regrowth leading to second-wave infection as the effect of cytokine induced reduction in immunological cell activation is diminished by IL-10 action. This mechanism is seen consistent with the clinical observation of late onset secondary infections in ICU patients frequently occur during the CARS phase and that IL-10 blockade in murine models improves late stage survival without worsening early cytokine storm severity [45].

### 4.3.3 Tissue Damage and the 'Point of No Return'
Mathematically, the self perpetuating nature of the cascading damage process is represented as a quadratic term (kD,comp × D2), which forms the basis for understanding the 'point of no return' in refractory sepsis shock patients. Damage associated molecular pattern molecules such as high mobility group box 1 protein (HMGB1), cell free DNA, and mitochondrial formyl peptides are secreted due to cellular death by necrosis and pyroptosis, thereby triggering sterile inflammation even in the absence of viable pathogens through the activation of Toll like receptors and the NLRP3 inflammasome [22,24]. The value of the threshold variable D = 0.36, from which point onwards the damage term becomes greater than the linear rate of formation and elimination, coincides biologically with the SOFA score value representing organ dysfunction, after which the death risk increases with additional organ involvement. From Figure 3, we can infer that even an instantaneous clearing of bacteria at the point of threshold crossing cannot stop the divergence of the solution trajectory, since once reached, the damage cascade is independent of bacteria. This mathematical result has shown a direct clinical relevance, it predicts that late antibiotic initiation (beyond approximately 18 to 24 hours in the invasive genotype) may achieve microbiological cure while failing to prevent fatal organ failure, consistent with multiple clinical trials demonstrating attenuated mortality benefit of antibiotics when initiated after sustained haemodynamic compromise [26].

### 4.3.4 Pharmacokinetics and Antibiotic Therapy
Both antibiotic therapies resulted in complete bacterial clearance despite challenging the identical invasive genotype under identical initial bacterial inoculum (B0=0.05). Nevertheless, ceftriaxone demonstrated a marginally lower maximum level of tissue damage compared to meropenem (Dmax= 0.247 vs 0.256). This unexpected result is driven purely by the differing pharmacokinetic dosing profiles of the two drugs within the model. The massive once-daily 60mg/L dose of ceftriaxone creates a distinct bactericidal spike (Cmax). By rapidly dropping the bacterial antigen load before the exponential DAMP generation cascade can peak, the large single dose minimises collateral damage slightly better than the less intensive, but more frequent, doses of meropenem. Ultimately, however, both regimens successfully suppressed inflammation below the critical D = 0.36 sepsis threshold, and the discrepancy between them (D=0.009) is negligible given the inherent uncertainties of the model parameters.
 
#### 4.3.4.1 Time vs. Concentration Dependency in PK/PD
This study is a direct investigation of the kinetics involved in the ongoing debate between time dependency versus concentration dependency in PK/PD parameters in sepsis pharmacology. Beta-lactams such as cephalosporins and carbapenems are known to be bactericidal via T>MIC kinetics rather than peak concentration (Cmax) kinetics [46]. The present study's use of the Hill function (E = Emax × Aeffn / [EC50n + Aeffn]), incorporates the T>MIC kinetics in the model used. In the case when Aeff substantially exceeds EC50 the equation tends to saturate, and thus, the interval when Aeff is lower than EC50 becomes the rate-limiting interval for bactericidal effect. Frequent administration of meropenem leads to a reduction of this rate-limiting interval which provides evidence for carbapenem extended or continuous infusion treatment strategy.
